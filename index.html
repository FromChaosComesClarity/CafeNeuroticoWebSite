what's in the cup

Three apps that used to ship separately, now one Electron binary with three faces — one runtime, one database, one download.

One library, every store

Steam, GOG, Epic, Flatpak, itch.io, PICO-8, emulators — and anything else you can start from a command line. Sign in to GOG and Epic without ever leaving the app.

Installs that stay in the app

GOG and Epic games download in-process with live progress, a queue and disk-space checks. Per-game Proton, prefixes and winetricks when you want them.

Artwork that never rots

Covers, hero art, logos, screenshots, HowLongToBeat, Metacritic and ProtonDB — scraped once and kept on your disk. No broken images when a storefront changes its mind.

Save games, backed up honestly

Find them, zip them, put them back. Portable archives that restore correctly on a different PC, and a snapshot taken before every restore.

A living room that isn't an afterthought

CREMA is fullscreen and gamepad-first: TV typography, immersive gamepages, achievements, a jukebox for your own music, a screensaver made of your own screenshots.

93 themes, 10 families

Catppuccin, Gruvbox, Nord, Dracula, Game Boy, Pip-Boy — and twenty retro operating systems, each with its own era typeface. Shared by all three faces.

Updates you asked for

One press checks your installed GOG and Epic games for new versions and updates them in place; Steam's pending downloads are flagged and handed to Steam. The app itself never phones home — it shows you its version and a link to the releases page, and the rest is your call.

Windows games that just run

Proton is found on your machine whatever the folder is called, and handed over explicitly — no guessing, no game that starts and silently isn't there. Don't have Proton? One click installs it. When something genuinely can't run, the app tells you why instead of shrugging.

Genres that mean something

Not "Action, Adventure, Indie" — ARPG, CRPG, FPS, Metroidvania, Soulslike, Shmup. Read from the tags players actually voted on, because that's the only source that can tell a Diablo-like from a Baldur's Gate-like. Filter by it, or let a playlist fill itself from it forever.

The manual, where the game kept it

RPGs still expect you to read something, and the PDF has usually been sitting in the game folder all along. One button finds it — GOG even names its own cluebooks and reference cards — or fetches the scanned original from the store. Keep as many as the game needs.

DOS games that actually start

GOG's DOS titles need a config its installer copies and a folder it starts them from; get either wrong and the window opens and closes. Both handled. Prefer a native DOSBox? It reads the very same GOG config, so every tweak made for that game survives.

Local-only. Yours.

Everything lives in one folder next to the AppImage. Copy it to a thumb drive and your library comes with you. No account, no telemetry, no cloud.

also on the menu

Separate apps, same roof. Cafe Neurotico is complete on its own — these are here if you want them.

OPTIONAL
EmuLatte
“I use RetroArch BTW”

The emulation pillar, and a fully standalone app in its own right — ROM library, emulator scanner, multi-source scraping and RetroAchievements. Use it entirely on its own, or drop it next to Cafe Neurotico and the suite picks it up automatically. Your ROM collection stays in EmuLatte: you pick which games cross over by exporting them to Cafe Neurotico from inside EmuLatte, and those arrive as ordinary entries under an Emulation category.
